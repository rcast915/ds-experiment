The L40S results validate the main practical claim of the DS-f32 compiler pass: on GPU hardware without native FP64 tensor cores, DS-f32 achieves near-f64 numerical accuracy while running at speeds approaching f32. All three automated test suites (dot product accuracy, matrix multiply accuracy, and float64-to-DS accuracy) passed on a Bridges-2 L40S node via Singularity container on 2026-06-26.

The L40S has no dedicated FP64 tensor cores. Its f64 throughput on CUDA cores is approximately 1/64 of its f32 throughput. This is the hardware regime where DS-f32 is most valuable, and where the H100 results (see ds_results_compiler_pass_h100.txt) could not demonstrate the full speedup because the H100 has native FP64 tensor cores running at ~51 TFLOPS.

For matrix multiply — the compute-bound workload where the f64 hardware bottleneck is most severe — the speedup of DS-f32 over native f64 scales steeply with problem size. At 256x256, DS-f32 is 1.21x faster than f64. At 512x512, it is 3.25x faster. At 1024x1024, it is 10.17x faster. At 2048x2048, DS-f32 takes 0.95 ms versus 12.19 ms for native f64 — a 12.87x speedup. In absolute throughput terms, the L40S delivers only ~1.4 TFLOPS for f64 GEMM versus ~18 TFLOPS for DS-f32 at 2048x2048. The DS matmul runs 4 f32 sub-matmuls internally, but f32 GEMM is so much faster on this hardware that even the fourfold decomposition cost is dwarfed by the f64 slowdown.

Element-wise and reduction workloads show little or no speedup: DS-f32 runs at 0.69x the speed of f64 for a 1M-element elementwise A*A+B, and at 0.92x the speed for a 1M-element reduction. These workloads are memory-bandwidth-bound. Both f64 and DS-f32 saturate the memory bus at similar rates — f64 reads 8 bytes per element while DS-f32 reads two f32 values for the same logical value — and the f64 CUDA-core bottleneck that dominates in matmul does not apply here. This is the expected behavior: the DS speedup is concentrated in compute-bound operations where the ratio of arithmetic to memory traffic is high.

The DS-f32 overhead relative to naive f32 on the L40S is consistent with the H100 measurements. For dot products, the mean overhead is 1.07x (max 1.26x across sizes 1K to 1M). For matrix multiply, the overhead grows with size because DS runs 4 sub-matmuls: it reaches 3.29x at 2048x2048 for (A*A)@B and 3.32x for standalone A@B. At smaller sizes (256x256 and below), the overhead is modest — 1.08x to 1.26x — because latency, not throughput, dominates and the 4-launch overhead is amortized across few FLOPs.

The numerical precision results match those observed on the H100. For a 10,000-element sum-of-squares dot product (`jnp.dot(a*a, b)`), DS-f32 error is 4.649e-06 versus f32 error of 7.927e-05 on the L40S. For f64 inputs (JAX_ENABLE_X64=1), DS-f32 sum error over 10,000 elements is 1.490e-05 versus f32 error of 1.221e-04 — consistent with the 8x improvement measured on the H100 and with the expected behavior of DS-f32's effective ~48-bit mantissa. The catastrophic cancellation test (triplet sum with 3333 groups of [1e8, 1.0, -1e8]) gives DS-f32 error 0.000e+00 versus f32 error 3.333e+03, confirming that the lo channel correctly accumulates rounding residuals through the compiled kernel on this hardware as well.

Taken together, the L40S results confirm that the DS-f32 compiler pass delivers its primary benefit on exactly the hardware where it is needed: GPUs where f64 is slow and users currently face a choice between f32 speed and f64 accuracy. On the L40S, DS-f32 eliminates that tradeoff for matmul-heavy workloads at sizes 512x512 and above, providing near-f64 accuracy (8x lower error than f32) while running an order of magnitude faster than native f64.
